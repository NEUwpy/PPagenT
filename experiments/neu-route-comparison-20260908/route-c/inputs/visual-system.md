# Visual system

## Visual thesis

Create a rational, typography-led consulting system in which structure carries
meaning. Use generous white space, disciplined grids, deep navy, cool gray, fine
rules, compact metadata, and one controlled accent.

The signature device is **title as analytical structure**: on selected pages, let the
main word or phrase participate in a pathway, matrix, funnel, threshold, network,
growth curve, or system architecture. Keep the word readable and the metaphor
semantically correct.

Do not turn every slide into a poster. Reserve the signature device for:

- cover;
- section dividers;
- one or two pivotal insight slides;
- closing synthesis.

Analysis pages should prioritize evidence, comparison, and decision clarity.

Use as the safe default for board, client, financial-services, and strategy work.

Use white or near-white pages, navy action titles, blue for a single focal series,
and cool gray for supporting context. Avoid dark backgrounds except for a cover or
section divider.

Use a compact title band, a large exhibit field, direct labels, numbered annotations,
methodology footnotes, and a consistent source band. Favor flat charts and exact
tables over hero numbers, cards, icons, and decorative frameworks. A typical page
contains one dominant exhibit plus one implication, not three equal panels.

Use the supplied report's chart grammar as the primary reference. Improve legibility
and editability, but do not erase material dimensions merely to create more white
space.

## Typography

Use fonts that are available on the target system. Prefer:

- Chinese sans: `PingFang SC`, `Microsoft YaHei`, `Noto Sans CJK SC`, or
  `Source Han Sans SC`;
- Chinese serif, only in Editorial Strategy: `Songti SC`, `Noto Serif CJK SC`, or
  `Source Han Serif SC`;
- Latin sans: `Aptos`, `Arial`, `Helvetica Neue`, or `Inter`;
- data and metadata: the same sans family with tabular figures; avoid a code font
  unless the subject genuinely requires it.

For a 16:9 deck, target:

| Role | Target size |
| --- | --- |
| Cover title | 48–66 pt |
| Signature word | 60–100 pt |
| Slide action title | 30–38 pt, never below the host's required minimum |
| Section title | 38–54 pt |
| Subhead or callout | 20–26 pt |
| Body | 16–20 pt |
| Chart label | 12–16 pt |
| Source note | 8–10 pt |

Shorten or split content before shrinking type. Keep a one-line title on one line.
Use at most three visible weights per slide. Small text must not use an ultra-light
weight.

## Grid and spacing

- Use a 12-column grid for standard slides.
- Use a 16-column grid only for Swiss Analytical pages that need precise subdivision.
- Keep equal left and right margins unless a deliberate full-bleed image breaks the
  grid.
- Reserve a consistent title band and source/footer band.
- Align text, charts, and annotations to shared axes.
- Use a 4 pt base spacing unit with common steps of 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, and 48 pt.
- Use hairlines of roughly 0.5–1 pt.
- Keep at least 0.4 in safe space from the slide edge for critical content.

Do not fill every region. White space must separate logical groups, not merely create
an expensive-looking void.

## Title-as-structure methods

Select one metaphor only.

| Meaning | Structural treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Growth | ascending path, compounding curve, widening channel |
| Precision | aperture, focus line, narrowing beam, target axis |
| Transformation | staged shift, bridge, state transition |
| System | modular architecture, connected nodes, layered stack |
| Risk | threshold, defense line, fault line, bounded zone |
| Market | coordinate space, window, competitive field |
| Value | staircase, exchange path, allocation flow |
| Conversion | narrowing funnel, gated path, selection field |

Make the geometry native and editable when practical. Do not rasterize critical title
text. Never distort characters until they become unreadable.

## Images

Use images only when they add evidence, atmosphere, or a concrete subject.

- Prefer documentary, architectural, industrial, or object-based imagery over stock
  handshakes and generic office teams.
- Match crop and subject placement to the slide layout.
- Use one dominant image rather than a collage unless comparison is the point.
- Avoid decorative AI imagery on analytical pages.
- Record asset sources in notes.

## Charts and diagrams

- Favor direct labels, restrained gridlines, and a highlighted conclusion.
- Use one dominant visual structure per slide.
- In Research Analytical mode, let the exhibit occupy roughly 55–75% of the usable
  canvas and keep sample, unit, date, and source visible.
- Reproduce material source exhibits with native charts or tables instead of
  converting them into metric cards.
- Keep simple diagrams flat and native.
- Avoid dashboard-like card grids, pills, badges, and UI chrome.
- Avoid icon collections as a substitute for analysis.

## Metadata

Use compact metadata only when it encodes real information:

- section;
- report category;
- date;
- confidentiality status;
- slide number;
- source.

Do not add arbitrary “01 / 02 / 03” markers when order has no meaning.

## Prohibited patterns

- blue-purple neon, cyberpunk glow, or rainbow gradients;
- full-slide dark backgrounds across the whole deck;
- thick rounded cards, glassmorphism, or app-dashboard styling;
- generic business people, handshakes, robots, or emoji;
- a large topic title with an unrelated icon beside it;
- multiple metaphors on one page;
- tiny poster-like copy;
- flattened text-heavy slide images;
- copied consultancy logos or a false claim of affiliation;
- “Powered by Peng” watermarks in another user's deck.
